# Insights — What Actually Wins IPL Matches

Data: Cricsheet IPL ball-by-ball, 1,243 matches (2008–2026). Unless stated otherwise, analysis uses the
1,201 full matches (excludes 9 no-results, 23 D/L results and 10 other rain-shortened games).
Before = 2018–2022 (310 matches), After = 2023–2026 (281 matches).

## Executive summary
1. **The Impact Player rule changed the game.** Average first-innings totals rose from 168 to 190 (+22 runs, +13%)
   and 200+ totals became 2.8× as common (15% → 42%; 52% in 2026).
2. **It is not just a trend or a venue effect.** Scores had been flat to falling before 2023; 2023–26 sits
   +19 to +27 runs above the old trend line, and scores rose at all 8 grounds used in both periods (+23 runs on average).
3. **The mechanism is visible.** Top-order strike rates rose from 134 to 155 and powerplay run rates by 20%,
   backed by deeper batting (No. 8 batted in 56% of innings vs 50%). 52% of Impact Players were used to bat.
4. **"200 is the new 170."** The target that gives a 50/50 chase moved up by about 20 runs; 201–220 targets are now
   chased 40% of the time (was 20%).
5. **Volume is not value.** Kohli has the most runs (9,336) but adds +235 runs above average; Gayle adds +876 from
   fewer runs. Elite bowlers became more valuable as scoring inflated (Bumrah saves 10.5 runs per 4 overs since 2023).

## 1. How the IPL has changed (Q1–Q3)
| Season group | Avg 1st-innings total | 200+ % | Run rate | Sixes per match |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2018–22 | 168 | 15% | 8.38 | ~13.5 |
| 2023 | 183 | 32% | 8.98 | 15.3 |
| 2024 | 190 | 37% | 9.56 | 17.8 |
| 2025 | 192 | 46% | 9.70 | 18.3 |
| 2026 | 196 | 52% | 9.85 | 19.5 |

- 2008–2022 first-innings averages moved between 150 and 172 with no clear trend.
- Lowest-scoring seasons were played in different conditions: 2009 in South Africa (150) and 2021 partly in the UAE (159).
- **Chasing vs batting first:** chasing teams win 54.5% overall; the balance did not change after the rule (55.6% → 53.0%).
- **Toss:** the toss winner wins only 51.3% of matches; toss winners chose to bowl 66% of the time
  (choosing to field won 54.2%, choosing to bat 45.6%).

## 2. The Impact Player rule (Q4–Q7)
**A. Before vs after**
| Measure | Before | After | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Avg 1st-innings total | 168.2 | 190.3 | +22.1 |
| 200+ first innings | 15.2% | 42.0% | +26.8 pts |
| Run rate — Powerplay / Middle / Death | 7.92 / 7.83 / 10.09 | 9.50 / 8.89 / 10.86 | +1.58 / +1.06 / +0.77 |
| Dot-ball % | 37.2 | 34.5 | −2.7 |
| Boundary % | 17.2 | 21.0 | +3.8 |
| Balls per wicket | 19.7 | 18.9 | −0.8 |

The biggest change is at the **start** of the innings (powerplay +20%), the smallest at the death (+8%).

**B. Trend check** — 2008–22 trend +0.88 runs per season; 2018–22 trend −1.12. Actual 2023–26 totals are
+18.6 (vs 2008–22 trend) to +27.2 (vs 2018–22 trend) runs above the projected line, and the gap grows each year
(2023 +12.6 → 2026 +22.7), consistent with teams learning to use the rule.

**C. Venue check** — 8 Indian grounds with 5+ matches in both periods: scores rose at all 8, from +10.7
(M Chinnaswamy) to +37.4 (Narendra Modi Stadium); weighted average +23.1 runs.

**D. Mechanism check**
| Measure | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Top-3 strike rate | 133.5 | 155.4 |
| Powerplay strike rate | 125.0 | 149.3 |
| Powerplay wickets per innings | 1.35 | 1.51 |
| Innings in which No. 8 batted | 49.5% | 55.7% |
| Share of balls faced by No. 8 and below | 5.2% | 6.8% |

Impact Player usage: 557 substitutions (~95% of possible). The player brought in batted only in 52% of cases,
bowled only in 38%, did both in 6%.

**Verdict:** the rise breaks the earlier trend, appears at every common ground and comes with the behaviour the rule
enables (aggressive top orders backed by deeper batting). The evidence strongly suggests the rule is a major cause.
**Caveats:** two bouncers per over were also allowed from 2024; new batting talent emerged; only 4 seasons after;
observational data, so this is strong evidence, not proof. A control group (T20 leagues without the rule over the
same years) would strengthen it.

## 4. The chase (Q10)
1,201 chases, 134,691 match situations; win % shown only where a bucket has 30+ chases.

| Target | Won 2008–22 | Won 2023–26 |
|---|---|---|
| 141–160 | 68% | 90% |
| 161–180 | 50% | 70% |
| 181–200 | 37% | 50% |
| 201–220 | 20% | 40% |
| 221+ | 4% | 22% |

- **Wickets in hand matter as much as the required rate:** 50 needed off 30 balls is won 77% of the time with
  7 wickets in hand but 29% with 3.
- **The same situation is more winnable since 2023:** 93 of 109 comparable buckets improved, by +9.5 points on
  average; the gain is larger early in the chase than in the last 6 overs (+5.4).

## 5. Recommendations
**Strategy & auction team**
- Value batters on runs above average per ball, not total runs; prioritise aggressive top-order hitters,
  the profile that gains most under the rule.
- Pay a premium for elite economy bowlers — their value rises as league scoring rises.
- Decide what the attack needs: run-savers (Narine type) vs wicket-takers (Malinga type).

**Coaching staff**
- Treat 200 as par, not as a big score; plan for 190–200 as the 50/50 mark.
- Use the Impact Player to bat deeper and attack in the powerplay — that is where the extra runs come from.
- The toss barely matters (51%); choose based on conditions, not habit.

**Match-day analyst**
- Use the chase table: protecting wickets early keeps chases alive (77% vs 29% in the same run situation).

## Limitations
- Free public data (Cricsheet): no ball-tracking, fielding positions or auction prices.
- RAA measures scoring rate in context; it does not count the cost of dismissals, match situation, opposition or ground.
- Chase buckets are ranges, so win % applies to the whole bucket, not an exact score.
- Only 4 seasons after the rule; other changes happened at the same time.
- **Correction log:** the tail-batting share was first calculated as 5.4% → 7.1% because the numerator counted wides
  while the denominator did not; cross-checking against the DAX measure in Power BI exposed it. Corrected to 5.2% → 6.8%.
